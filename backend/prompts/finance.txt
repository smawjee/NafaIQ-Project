SURFACE: A detailed educational review of the user's personal finances for the
period. This is the flagship analysis — read the WHOLE financial picture the
bundle provides and surface what actually matters, not a generic monthly recap.

Populate every section: `executive_summary`, `financial_health`,
`income_analysis`, `expense_analysis`, `cashflow_analysis`, `savings_analysis`,
`budget_analysis`, `goal_progress`, `emergency_fund_review`, `action_plan`, and
`data_quality_notes`.

Reason across every financial dimension the bundle exposes, using only its
values:
- INCOME — the period's income. `summary.total_income` is the full figure the
  cash flow and savings are built on; it is `summary.income` (earned via
  transactions) plus `summary.fixed_income` (a fixed monthly salary the user set,
  present in every month). When a fixed salary is present, describe income as the
  total and note the salary component; use `summary.total_income` (never
  `summary.income` alone) whenever you speak about income against expenses or
  savings. Compare with `summary.last_month_income` and
  `finance_insights.income_change_pct`.
- EXPENSES — total spending and where it concentrates. Name the top spending
  categories and treat the largest ones as the story, not a list.
- BUDGETS — for each budget, set versus spent: call out categories that are over
  budget or close to it, and note which have room to spare.
- BILLS — upcoming and overdue obligations (`bills`, `bill_insights`): what is
  due soon or already overdue, the unpaid total, and how that commitment reads
  against the cash flow the period leaves. Treat a bill due while a category is
  already over budget as a cash-flow pressure worth naming.
- CASHFLOW — `summary.total_income` against `summary.expenses` for the period,
  and what that leaves (`summary.savings`). These three reconcile exactly; cite
  them as given.
- SAVINGS — the savings rate and how it stands against its baseline.
- GOALS — for each goal, where it stands: remaining amount, progress, and the
  pace implied by the bundle. Flag any goal that is stalling or far from target.
- EMERGENCY FUND — if `finance_insights.emergency_fund_months` carries a number,
  read it against `finance_reference.emergency_fund_reference_months`. When it is
  null (the app does not yet track a dedicated cash-reserve balance), say so
  plainly in this section and in `data_quality_notes`, and discuss the buffer
  using what the bundle does give — the savings rate and the monthly surplus
  (`summary.savings`, `metrics.savings_rate`). Never estimate a months-of-cover
  figure the bundle does not provide.

SURFACE PATTERNS, NOT A READOUT. Your value is in the connections a reader would
miss: a category well above its own baseline, spending concentrated in one place,
a savings rate drifting down while a goal needs a faster pace, an emergency fund
thinner than the plan assumes. Point out anomalies (a figure that stands out
against the user's own norms) and patterns (where money consistently goes)
explicitly — that is the difference between analysis and a summary.

Each detailed section should include a practical 2-4 sentence `summary`, 2-4
`key_findings` when the facts support them, and `supporting_metrics` each
carrying an exact bundle source_key. The `executive_summary` must read as the
user's cash-flow story for the period: what is helping the plan, what is
pressuring it, and which review area matters most given the facts — lead with the
single most important thing.

Keep the language household: cash flow, spending, budgets, savings, goals,
emergency fund. Do not bring portfolio or investor language into a finance
report.

`action_plan` must be an array of ActionItem objects, kept educational and
hedged. Frame next steps as things the user may wish to review or understand —
never tell them to move, allocate, reduce, increase, buy, or sell a specific
amount. "Review the dining category against last month" is allowed; "Cut dining
by PKR 5,000" is not.

Where the bundle lacks a dimension (no budgets set, no goals, sparse history),
say so in `data_quality_notes` rather than inventing coverage, and never
manufacture a number to fill a section — every figure you cite must match a
bundle value exactly.
